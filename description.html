viz-man

A full-screen MilkDrop visualizer for Music Assistant. It plays no audio: it draws what your speakers are playing, in time with it, from the waveform Music Assistant's MilkDrop Visualizer plugin streams a few seconds ahead.

What it does

  • Follows whichever speaker is playing, or the one you pick; sync groups are resolved the way Music Assistant does.
  • Ships all 395 presets from the butterchurn-presets packs. Scan presets measures each one's frame rate on your TV (about 35 minutes, once) and builds a validated list of the ones it runs smoothly; add or remove presets by hand.
  • Favorites, shared with Music Assistant's own MilkDrop favorites and between your TVs.
  • Random order with a timer, or stay on the preset you chose.
  • Shows the track (artist, title, album, label) when it starts, always, or never. Dims the picture and the track card independently for the evening.
  • Color: album art gives each preset the hue of the track's artwork, from the palette Music Assistant sends with the stream, at the brightness the preset meant; a strength setting goes with it.
  • Keeps the screensaver off while music plays; nudges the picture against OLED burn-in.

Requirements

  • Music Assistant 2.10 or newer, on the same network, with the MilkDrop Visualizer plugin added (Settings → Plugins).
  • Speakers Music Assistant streams to itself (Sonos, Chromecast, AirPlay, Squeezelite, …). A speaker playing something on its own, such as Spotify straight from the Spotify app, carries nothing it can draw.
  • A TV with WebGL2 (LG's 2020 models onward, many earlier). Tested on an LG CX (webOS 5). No root required.

Setting up

On first launch, sign in with Music Assistant's address and your username and password. The TV gets its own long-lived token, named after it, which you can revoke in Music Assistant's settings or with Log out in the menu; the password is not stored. Then run Scan presets once. The setup guide walks through everything; the preset scan explains how the validated list works.
